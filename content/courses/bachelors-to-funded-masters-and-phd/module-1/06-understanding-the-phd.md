# Understanding the PhD

## What is a PhD?

A Doctor of Philosophy (PhD) is an advanced academic degree awarded by a university after a student completes an independent and original research project that contributes new knowledge to a particular field of study. Unlike a Bachelor's or Master's degree, which often involves structured classes, examinations and coursework, a PhD focuses mainly on research. Students identify a research problem, review existing literature, develop research questions, collect and analyse evidence, and present their findings in a doctoral thesis or dissertation.

A PhD also involves working with academic supervisors who guide the research process. At the end of the programme, candidates usually defend their research before an academic examination panel through an oral examination, commonly called a *viva voce* or thesis defence.

A PhD is suitable for people who want to develop advanced research expertise, pursue academic careers, contribute to specialist fields or work in research-intensive organisations.
