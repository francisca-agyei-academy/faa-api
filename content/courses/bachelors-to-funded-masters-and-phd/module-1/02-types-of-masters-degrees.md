# Understanding the Different Types of Master's Degrees

One important thing prospective applicants should understand is that Master's programmes are not identical. Universities offer different postgraduate qualifications depending on the academic discipline, professional objectives and research expectations of the programme. Some programmes focus primarily on coursework and professional development, while others place considerable emphasis on independent research.

Master's programmes also differ in duration. Depending on the country, university, programme structure and study mode, some Master's degrees take one year to complete, while others take two years or longer. For example, a programme offered over one year in one country might have a two-year equivalent in another country. Part-time study might also extend the duration.

Part-time study refers to a mode of study in which students take fewer subjects or courses per semester than students enrolled in full-time study. As a result, they spread their academic workload over a longer period and take more time to complete their degree. Understanding these differences is important because the structure, duration and research requirements of your Master's programme might influence your eligibility for future PhD opportunities, professional development and career progression.

## 1.2.1 Coursework-based Master's degree

A coursework-based Master's degree primarily involves structured learning through lectures, seminars, tutorials, assignments, examinations and group projects. Students complete a prescribed set of subjects designed to develop advanced knowledge and specialised skills. Some coursework programmes include a research project or dissertation, while others focus mainly on taught courses.

A coursework-based Master's degree is particularly suitable for students who want to deepen their knowledge in a specific discipline, develop specialised professional skills and strengthen their qualifications for career advancement. Although these programmes primarily focus on structured learning, some also provide opportunities to develop research skills through projects and dissertations. The suitability of a coursework-based Master's degree ultimately depends on your academic interests, professional ambitions and the knowledge and skills you intend to acquire through postgraduate education.

**Typical characteristics of a coursework-based Master's include:**

- Structured academic timetable.
- Prescribed subjects and units.
- Lectures, seminars and tutorials.
- Individual and group assignments.
- Examinations and presentations.
- Possible research project or dissertation.

## 1.2.2 Research-based Master's degree

A research-based Master's degree places greater emphasis on independent academic investigation. Instead of spending most of the programme attending structured classes, students undertake a research project under the guidance of an academic supervisor. The purpose is to develop the ability to identify research problems, examine existing literature, formulate research questions, select appropriate methodologies, collect and analyse evidence, and present research findings.

The exact structure and research expectations vary across institutions and countries. A Master of Philosophy (MPhil), for example, is not structured identically everywhere. A research-based Master's degree is particularly valuable for applicants considering a PhD because it provides opportunities to develop research independence, academic writing, methodological competence and experience in producing an original research project. Students develop experience in academic writing, independent investigation, research design, and critical analysis. These experiences help demonstrate readiness for doctoral study.

**Examples of research-based Master's degrees include:** Master of Philosophy (MPhil), Master of Research (MRes), Master by Research, Master of Science by Research and Master of Arts by Research.

**Typical characteristics of a research-based Master's degree:**

- Independent research project.
- Academic supervision.
- Extensive literature review.
- Research methodology development.
- Data collection and analysis.
- Thesis or dissertation preparation.
- Research presentations and examination.

## 1.2.3 Mixed-mode Master's degree

A mixed-mode Master's degree combines structured academic coursework with independent research. Students undertake taught subjects while also completing a research project, dissertation or thesis as part of their programme. This structure allows students to develop advanced knowledge in their chosen discipline while gaining practical experience in academic research.

Unlike coursework-based programmes, which primarily focus on taught subjects, or research-based programmes, which primarily focus on research, mixed-mode programmes allocate a substantial part of the degree to independent research. At the same time, they differ from research-based programmes by requiring students to complete a structured set of academic subjects alongside their research activities.

**Typical characteristics include:**

- Structured academic coursework.
- Research methodology training.
- Independent research project or dissertation.
- Academic supervision and guidance.
- Literature review and research design.
- Data collection and analysis, where applicable.
- A combination of coursework assignments, examinations and research assessment.

**Examples of qualifications that might follow a mixed-mode structure include:**

- Master of Science (MSc) with a substantial research dissertation.
- Master of Arts (MA) with an independent research project.
- Master of Philosophy (MPhil) with both coursework and research components, where offered in this format.
- Other discipline-specific Master's degrees combining taught subjects with independent research.

The Master of Research (MRes) is also worth mentioning, although it is generally more research-intensive and its structure differs across universities. It should not automatically be classified as a mixed-mode qualification.

A mixed-mode Master's degree is particularly suitable for students who want to develop advanced subject knowledge while gaining research experience. It offers an opportunity to strengthen academic writing, research methodology, critical analysis and independent investigation skills. This combination is valuable for students who wish to pursue further academic studies, including doctoral research, as well as those seeking professional careers that require research and analytical competencies.
