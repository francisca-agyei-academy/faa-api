# How Long Does a PhD Take?

PhD duration depends on the country, university, discipline, study mode and research requirements. The following table provides general guidance. These are typical planning ranges, not guaranteed programme durations.

| Country or region | Typical full-time PhD duration |
| --- | --- |
| Australia | 3–4 years |
| United Kingdom | 3–4 years |
| United States | 4–6 years |
| Canada | 4–6 years |
| Germany | 3–5 years |
| Netherlands | 4 years |
| Switzerland | 3–5 years |
| Sweden | 4 years |
| Norway | 3–4 years |
| Finland | 3–4 years |
| France | 3 years |
| Spain | 3–4 years |
| Italy | 3 years |
| South Africa | 3–5 years |
| Ghana | 3–5 years |
| Nigeria | 3–5 years |
| Japan | 3 years after a Master's degree |
| South Korea | 3–5 years |
| China | 3–4 years |
| Malaysia | 3–5 years |
| New Zealand | 3–4 years |

Part-time PhD programmes often take approximately 5–8 years, although the permitted maximum varies by university.

> **Important:** Always check the official programme page. Some universities specify a minimum registration period, a maximum completion period or different timelines for international students.
