# What Do Universities Look for in Bachelor's Graduates Applying for Master's Degrees?

Having a Bachelor's degree does not automatically guarantee admission into a Master's programme. Universities assess applicants based on their academic qualifications, previous academic performance, relevant background, and suitability for the programme they intend to pursue.

Admission requirements differ across universities, countries and academic disciplines. Some programmes accept applicants from a broad range of undergraduate backgrounds, while others require specific prerequisite subjects, professional experience or relevant academic preparation.

## Assessment criteria most universities use to screen applicants

| Assessment criteria | What universities want to establish |
| --- | --- |
| Academic qualifications | Whether you hold a recognised Bachelor's degree or equivalent qualification. |
| Academic performance | Whether your grades meet the university's minimum entry requirements. |
| Relevant academic background | Whether your undergraduate discipline aligns with the intended Master's programme. |
| Prerequisite subjects | Whether you have completed specific courses or acquired the necessary foundational knowledge. |
| Professional experience | Whether you possess relevant work experience, particularly for professional programmes such as an MBA. |
| Statement of purpose | Whether you have clear academic interests and reasons for choosing the programme. |
| English language proficiency | Whether you meet the university's language requirements, where applicable. |
| Academic references | Whether referees support your academic ability and suitability for postgraduate study, where required. |
| Portfolio or additional requirements | Whether you meet discipline-specific requirements, such as a design portfolio, interview or professional registration. |
| Curriculum Vitae (CV) | Your academic background, research experience, employment history, internships, leadership activities, skills and relevant achievements. |
| Publications (if available and applicable) | Whether you have contributed to academic publications, journal articles, book chapters or conference papers. Publications are not compulsory for most Master's applications, but they demonstrate research engagement. |

You do not always need a Bachelor's degree in exactly the same discipline as your intended Master's programme. Some universities provide alternative admission pathways for applicants who do not meet the standard academic entry requirements. These pathways might consider relevant professional experience, additional qualifications, or preparatory programmes. Availability depends on the institution and programme.

A strong academic record improves your competitiveness, particularly for scholarship opportunities. Meeting the minimum requirements establishes your eligibility to apply, while a strong academic and professional profile improves your competitiveness. Some programmes require additional documents, examinations, or interviews.

> **NB:** Before applying for a Master's programme, carefully examine the admission requirements of each university. Pay attention to the required undergraduate qualification, minimum grades, prerequisite subjects, language requirements and any additional selection criteria. Your objective should be to identify programmes that match your academic background, career ambitions and future research interests.

## Learners must understand that

- Meeting university admission requirements does not guarantee a scholarship.
- Receiving an admission offer does not automatically mean tuition and living expenses are covered.
- Scholarship applicants often face additional selection criteria.
- Some scholarships require separate applications, while others consider applicants automatically.
